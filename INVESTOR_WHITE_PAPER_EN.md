# 1. Executive Summary

Every year, thousands of couples from around the world choose Italy as the destination for one of the most important days of their lives.

They dream of getting married on the shores of Lake Como, among the vineyards of Tuscany, on the Amalfi Coast or in the countryside of Puglia. Yet, behind this dream lies a complex planning process that often lasts more than a year and requires coordinating dozens of independent suppliers across different languages, time zones and communication channels.

Today, organizing a destination wedding in Italy remains surprisingly inefficient.

Couples spend months searching for venues, contacting wedding planners, requesting quotations, comparing offers, coordinating hotels, transportation and guest experiences. Most of these activities still happen through emails, WhatsApp messages, spreadsheets and PDF documents, making the entire process fragmented, stressful and difficult to manage.

On the other side, wedding planners and suppliers face the same inefficiencies. Every new inquiry requires gathering information, rewriting proposals, answering repetitive questions, translating conversations and coordinating multiple stakeholders manually. Valuable time is spent on administrative work instead of delivering exceptional customer experiences.

The wedding industry has embraced digital marketing, but it has not yet embraced digital operations.

White-Rose.ai was created to solve this problem.

Rather than building another marketplace where couples browse endless lists of suppliers, White-Rose.ai creates a collaborative digital platform where couples and wedding professionals work together throughout the entire planning journey.

The experience starts with an AI Wedding Concierge.

Through a natural conversation, the platform understands the couple's vision, budget, destination, style, guest list and logistical requirements. Artificial intelligence then transforms these conversations into a structured Wedding Brief that can be shared with the most suitable partners within the White-Rose network.

Instead of forcing couples to contact dozens of suppliers individually, White-Rose.ai brings the right professionals directly into the project.

Wedding planners, venues, hotels, photographers, florists, caterers and other partners receive the brief, prepare tailored proposals and communicate with clients inside a single collaborative workspace.

Artificial intelligence continues supporting both sides throughout the process.

For couples, it answers questions, organizes information, monitors budgets, summarizes conversations and keeps the planning process under control.

For professionals, it assists in preparing quotations, drafting responses, translating conversations, organizing customer requests and reducing repetitive administrative work.

The objective is not to replace wedding planners or suppliers.

The objective is to make every professional dramatically more efficient while creating a significantly better experience for couples.

This human-centered approach differentiates White-Rose.ai from traditional AI products.

Artificial intelligence does not replace expertise.

It amplifies it.

The company launches in Italy for two strategic reasons.

First, Italy is one of the world's most prestigious destination wedding markets, attracting international couples willing to invest significantly more than the average domestic wedding.

Second, the founding team already benefits from an established network of wedding planners, venues and hospitality partners, allowing White-Rose.ai to build a high-quality supply network from day one without relying on cold acquisition.

This combination of technology and industry relationships creates a unique opportunity to become the digital infrastructure of the Italian destination wedding ecosystem.

The initial business model is intentionally simple.

The platform will be free for both couples and partners, eliminating every barrier to adoption during the early growth phase.

Revenue will be generated through a commission on successfully completed weddings booked through the platform, aligning the company's interests with those of both customers and suppliers.

As more weddings are organized through White-Rose.ai, the platform generates increasing Gross Merchandise Value (GMV), while every completed project enriches the knowledge base, improves AI assistance and strengthens the partner network.

Over time, White-Rose.ai will evolve from an intelligent planning platform into the operating system that powers the entire lifecycle of destination weddings.

The long-term vision extends beyond weddings themselves.

The same collaborative infrastructure can support luxury travel experiences, anniversaries, private celebrations, corporate events and other premium experiences where multiple suppliers, customers and hospitality operators must work together.

White-Rose.ai is not building another wedding marketplace.

It is building the infrastructure that allows the world's most memorable events to be imagined, planned and delivered through a single intelligent platform.

# 3. The Problem

A typical destination wedding involves:

* a wedding planner
* one or more venues
* accommodation providers
* caterers
* photographers
* videographers
* florists
* musicians
* transportation services
* beauty professionals
* guest experiences
* legal documentation
* local logistics

Each supplier operates independently.

Each conversation starts from scratch.

Each quotation arrives in a different format.

Information is duplicated across emails, chat messages, documents and spreadsheets.

As the number of suppliers increases, complexity grows exponentially.

Small changes—such as increasing the guest count, changing the ceremony time or modifying the floral design—often require contacting multiple suppliers individually and requesting updated quotations.

There is no single source of truth.

Wedding planners spend countless hours collecting information, preparing proposals, answering repetitive questions and coordinating communication between independent businesses.

Most of this work creates no additional value.

It is simply administrative overhead.

As the business grows, professionals can only scale by hiring more people.

The entire industry suffers from the same structural problem.

Relationships are managed manually.

Information is fragmented.

Coordination depends almost entirely on human effort.

## Success-based revenue

Revenue is generated only when a wedding is successfully organized through the platform.

When suppliers are confirmed and the wedding moves forward, White-Rose.ai receives a commission based on the total value of the services booked.

The expected commission ranges between **3% and 7%**, depending on the type of services involved.

The long-term target is an average take rate of approximately **5%**.

For example:

€60,000

Platform commission:

5%

White-Rose.ai revenue:

€3,000

The business model aligns incentives across the entire ecosystem.

Couples pay nothing to access the platform.

Partners pay nothing to join.

White-Rose.ai earns revenue only after creating measurable value for both sides.

# 15. Risks & Mitigation

Every ambitious company faces execution risks.

The objective is not to eliminate uncertainty.

The objective is to understand it and prepare for it.

---

## Marketplace Risk

Like every marketplace, White-Rose.ai must simultaneously grow demand and supply.

Mitigation:

The company launches with an existing professional network and focuses initially on one highly concentrated market.

---

## Operational Risk

The quality of the customer experience depends on the quality of suppliers.

Mitigation:

The platform prioritizes curated onboarding over rapid expansion, ensuring only trusted professionals join the ecosystem.

---

## Technology Risk

Artificial intelligence evolves rapidly.

Foundational AI models will become increasingly accessible to competitors.

Mitigation:

White-Rose.ai does not compete on AI models.

It competes on workflow, operational knowledge, relationships and marketplace execution.

---

## Expansion Risk

Entering multiple countries too early increases operational complexity.

Mitigation:

The company will dominate one market before replicating the model internationally.

Geographic expansion follows operational maturity rather than ambition alone.
